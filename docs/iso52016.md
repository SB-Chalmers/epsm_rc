# ISO 52016-1 hourly model

The hourly method in ISO 52016-1:2017 Clause 6.5 is the default thermal model
(`ThermalModel.ISO52016_1`, `thermal_model="iso52016_1"`).
`thermal_model="iso13790_5r1c"` selects the ISO 13790 5R1C network instead.
Everything other than the thermal network is shared with 5R1C: weather, solar
on each orientation, context shading, window optics, internal gains, airflow,
thermostat schedules, ground reduction and result channels. A difference between
the two models is therefore a difference in the network alone.

```python
from epsm_rc import RCSettings, simulate_building

settings = RCSettings(
    airflow_policy="sensible_only_unlimited_supply",
    ground_policy="iso13370_periodic_approximation",
    iso13370_wall_thickness_m=0.3,
    iso13370_wall_thickness_source="ASSUMED",
)
result = simulate_building(building, weather=weather, settings=settings)
```

## Network

- Each opaque element group has five nodes. Conductances between nodes are
  6/R, 3/R, 3/R and 6/R, and the capacity κ_m is distributed by mass class
  (Clause 6.5.7.2). Windows have two nodes and no capacity.
- Elements are grouped by facade, construction and boundary. R and κ_m come from
  the Core layers. The standard leaves the mass class to the user. EPSM takes the
  layer with the largest resistance as the insulation: when it holds no more
  than half the total resistance the class is D, otherwise the share of capacity
  on its room side selects I (≥ 80 %), E (≤ 20 %) or IE.
- Inside surfaces use the Table 25 coefficients: h_ci 5.0 (upwards), 2.5
  (horizontal) or 0.7 (downwards), and h_ri 5.13, exchanged with an
  area-weighted mean radiant temperature. Exterior surfaces use h_ce 20 and
  h_re 4.14 plus a sky term F_sky·h_re·Δθ_er. The sky view factors and Δθ_er
  come from `RCSettings`.
- Opaque solar is absorbed at the exterior node, with absorptance taken from the
  outer Core material. Transmitted window solar is shared 10 % to the air and
  90 % to inside surfaces by area. Internal gains are 40 % convective, and HVAC
  is fully convective (Annex B).
- The air node holds air plus 10 kJ/(m² K) of internal capacity per floor area.
  Thermal bridges (Core uplifts) connect outdoor air to the air node as H_tb.
- Intermediate floors and partitions inside the zone are split at the mid-plane
  into two adiabatic halves (floor face and ceiling face).
- A conditioned basement and each cold attic are further zones of the same
  network (multi-zone method, Clause 6.5.5.1). The floor over the basement and
  the attic ceiling are single five-node elements whose surface nodes sit in
  the two zones, each face with its own Table 25 coefficients and mean radiant
  temperature. The basement keeps its own gains, airflow and thermostat. The
  attic is unconditioned: its air node has no furniture capacity, its outdoor
  air exchange is the declared `UnconditionedExchangeSpec`, and its roof and
  gable faces get their own tilted-plane solar and sky forcing. This is how
  EnergyPlus models both spaces (`cold_attic_policy="unconditioned_zone"`).
- Ground with `iso13370_periodic_approximation` adds the Clause 6.5.8 soil layer
  (0.5 m, λ 2.0, ρc 2.0 MJ/(m³ K)) and a virtual layer. Together they make the
  steady path equal to the ISO 13370 conductance. The chain uses the
  construction resistance, not R_fl;eff; a literal reading of the Eq. (48) terms
  would count the ground twice. Prescribed monthly temperatures use the
  construction U-value. `conductance_multiplier` adds an outdoor-air resistance
  so that the element conductance equals b·U.

Each hour is solved implicitly. Because the network is linear, the free-floating
state plus the response to unit HVAC power gives the exact thermostat power,
which is the Clause 6.5.5.2 interpolation without trial runs. With a
conditioned basement the two thermostats are coupled; the solver takes the
first consistent combination of free, at-setpoint and at-capacity per zone. The
matrix is inverted once; the hourly ventilation conductances enter through the
Woodbury identity (a rank-one update for a single zone).
`control_temperature="air"` (default) controls air temperature, as EnergyPlus
ideal loads do. `"operative"` controls operative temperature, as the standard
specifies; it adds about 5 % heating relative to EnergyPlus. The year is run twice,
and the second pass is reported, so initial node temperatures do not bias the result.

## Heat recovery

`heat_recovery="when_beneficial"` (default) works with either model, in Python
and Rust and for coupled basement domains. It applies sensible recovery only
when it reduces the load. Recovery is on in
heating mode when outdoor air is colder than the zone, and in cooling mode when
it is warmer. The mode is taken from the previous hour: heating or cooling power
was delivered, or the air temperature was at or beyond a setpoint, so an unheated
zone that needs heat still counts as heating. All other hours bypass the
exchanger. This mirrors the EnergyPlus ideal-loads rule with a one-hour lag.
Comparisons use tolerances of 1e-6 K and 1e-3 W so the Python and Rust loops take
the same decision when air sits on a setpoint or equals the outdoor temperature.
With `heat_recovery="always"`, summer ventilation loses most of its free cooling, which made both RC
models overpredict cooling strongly in the EnergyPlus comparison
(`benchmarks/iso52016/`).

## Default choices

Every physics choice is a named setting with a string enum in `epsm_rc.options`
(`list(WindowOptics)` shows the options). The defaults are the options that agreed
best with EnergyPlus 24.2 ideal loads in `benchmarks/iso52016/`: 38 Studio-style
Gothenburg cases plus 144 stress cases in six climates, with cooling compared on
the sensible coil energy.

| Setting | Default | Alternative | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `thermal_model` | `iso52016_1` | `iso13790_5r1c` | Free-float RMSE 0.84 K vs 1.21 K; cooling 1.00 vs 1.17 × E+ |
| `control_temperature` | `air` | `operative` | Heating 1.06 vs 1.11 × E+; cooling 1.00 vs 1.10 × E+ |
| `window_optics` | `angular` | `constant_shgc` | Cooling ratio drops by about a third with angular optics |
| `solar_time` | `midpoint` | `hour_start` | EPW irradiance is an hourly average; midpoint matches it |
| `heat_recovery` | `when_beneficial` | `always` | Removes the 3–7 × cooling overprediction |

The former profiles map to explicit choices: `epsm_rc_5r1c_v1` is 5R1C with
constant SHGC, hour-start solar time and recovery always on; `epsm_rc_5r1c_v2`
adds angular optics and midpoint solar time. `RCSettings.from_policy` and the
keywords `window_optics_method`, `solar_time_reference`, `heat_recovery_control`
and `iso52016_control_temperature` still work and emit a `DeprecationWarning`.

## Limits

Unconditioned basements, attic glazing and attics with vertical interfaces to
other zones are rejected, as under 5R1C. The hourly recurrence runs in the Rust
extension when it is installed (about 25 ms per year) and otherwise in numpy
(about 0.3 s); weather and solar preparation dominate the total run time. Moisture (Clause 6.5.14) is not modelled. Results
are model outputs, not validation against measured buildings.
