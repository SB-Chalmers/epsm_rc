# ISO 52016-1 hourly model

`RCSettings(thermal_model="iso52016_1_hourly")` selects the hourly method in
ISO 52016-1:2017 Clause 6.5 instead of the ISO 13790 5R1C network. The named
policy `epsm_rc_iso52016_v1` selects it together with the v2 solar defaults.
Everything other than the thermal network is shared with 5R1C: weather, solar
on each orientation, context shading, window optics, internal gains, airflow,
thermostat schedules, ground reduction and result channels. A difference between
the two models is therefore a difference in the network alone.

```python
from epsm_rc import RCSettings, simulate_building

settings = RCSettings.from_policy(
    "epsm_rc_5r1c_v1",
    thermal_model="iso52016_1_hourly",
    airflow_policy="sensible_only_unlimited_supply",
    ground_policy="iso13370_periodic_approximation",
    iso13370_wall_thickness_m=0.3,
    iso13370_wall_thickness_source="ASSUMED",
)
result = simulate_building(building, weather=weather, settings=settings)
```

## Network

- Each opaque element group has five nodes. Conductances between nodes are
  6/R, 3/R, 3/R and 6/R, and the capacity κ_m is distributed by mass class
  (Clause 6.5.7.2). Windows have two nodes and no capacity.
- Elements are grouped by facade, construction and boundary. R and κ_m come from
  the Core layers. The standard leaves the mass class to the user. EPSM takes the
  layer with the largest resistance as the insulation: when it holds no more
  than half the total resistance the class is D, otherwise the share of capacity
  on its room side selects I (≥ 80 %), E (≤ 20 %) or IE.
- Inside surfaces use the Table 25 coefficients: h_ci 5.0 (upwards), 2.5
  (horizontal) or 0.7 (downwards), and h_ri 5.13, exchanged with an
  area-weighted mean radiant temperature. Exterior surfaces use h_ce 20 and
  h_re 4.14 plus a sky term F_sky·h_re·Δθ_er. The sky view factors and Δθ_er
  come from `RCSettings`.
- Opaque solar is absorbed at the exterior node, with absorptance taken from the
  outer Core material. Transmitted window solar is shared 10 % to the air and
  90 % to inside surfaces by area. Internal gains are 40 % convective, and HVAC
  is fully convective (Annex B).
- The air node holds air plus 10 kJ/(m² K) of internal capacity per floor area.
  Thermal bridges (Core uplifts) connect outdoor air to the air node as H_tb.
- Intermediate floors and partitions inside the zone are split at the mid-plane
  into two adiabatic halves (floor face and ceiling face).
- Ground with `iso13370_periodic_approximation` adds the Clause 6.5.8 soil layer
  (0.5 m, λ 2.0, ρc 2.0 MJ/(m³ K)) and a virtual layer. Together they make the
  steady path equal to the ISO 13370 conductance. The chain uses the
  construction resistance, not R_fl;eff; a literal reading of the Eq. (48) terms
  would count the ground twice. Prescribed monthly temperatures use the
  construction U-value. `conductance_multiplier` adds an outdoor-air resistance
  so that the element conductance equals b·U.

Each hour is solved implicitly. Because the network is linear, the free-floating
state plus the response to unit HVAC power gives the exact thermostat power,
which is the Clause 6.5.5.2 interpolation without trial runs. The matrix is
inverted once; the hourly ventilation conductance enters as a rank-one update.
The standard controls operative temperature; `iso52016_control_temperature="air"`
controls air temperature, as EnergyPlus ideal loads do. The year is run twice,
and the second pass is reported, so initial node temperatures do not bias the result.

## Heat-recovery bypass

`heat_recovery_control="load_dependent_bypass"` works with either model. It
applies sensible recovery only when it reduces the load. Recovery is on in an
hour that follows heating when outdoor air is colder than the zone, or follows
cooling when it is warmer. All other hours bypass the exchanger. This mirrors the
EnergyPlus ideal-loads rule with a one-hour lag. With the default `continuous`
recovery, summer ventilation loses most of its free cooling, which made both RC
models overpredict cooling strongly in the EnergyPlus comparison
(`benchmarks/iso52016/`).

## Limits

The current version supports single-domain buildings. Conditioned basements and
cold attics are rejected under the ISO 52016-1 model, and heat-recovery bypass is
rejected for conditioned basements. The model runs in Python/numpy, about 1 s per
year for typical buildings. Moisture (Clause 6.5.14) is not modelled. Results
are model outputs, not validation against measured buildings.
